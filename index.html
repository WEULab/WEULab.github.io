Current Products

Initial development focuses on short-range, high-resolution precipitation and temperature uncertainty.

Precipitation Uncertainty

Subhourly ensemble guidance showing the probability, expected intensity, and range of possible precipitation outcomes at 15-minute intervals.

  • 15-minute forecast intervals
  • High-resolution local grids
  • Probability, ensemble mean, and maximum intensity
  • Short-range weather uncertainty

Temperature Uncertainty

Local temperature ensemble guidance designed to show not only the expected temperature, but also the spread and variability among possible forecast outcomes.

  • Subhourly temperature guidance
  • Time-lagged ensemble information
  • Neighborhood-scale visualization
  • Consistent uncertainty display

Why Uncertainty?

A single deterministic forecast cannot describe every plausible weather outcome. Ensemble information provides a range of possible conditions and helps users understand where forecasts are stable, where they are changing, and where uncertainty is greatest.

Future Directions

Extreme Weather

Localized information for heavy precipitation, snow, temperature extremes, wind, visibility, and other high-impact weather conditions.

Flood & Water Hazards

Extending probabilistic weather information toward runoff, flash-flood, and other hydrologic hazard applications.

Observation Updates

Combining numerical forecasts with surface observations, radar, and potentially vehicle or other high-frequency sensors to update local conditions more frequently.

Decision Support

Translating weather uncertainty into information useful for transportation, autonomous systems, infrastructure, emergency response, and other weather-sensitive operations.

Research & Development

WEUL is currently an independent research and development effort. Early products use operational numerical weather prediction data and ensemble techniques to investigate practical approaches for high-resolution probabilistic forecasting.

Development is ongoing. Products shown here are experimental and are not intended for operational warning or safety-critical use.
